Testing, not proof. This page logs every solar wind speed spike, every solar-wind lull (a sharp drop, or the quiet after a geomagnetic storm), and every U.S. volcano alert-level change, using rules fixed before looking at the results. A spike match only means the two happened within 48 hours of each other. A lull is flagged if a quake cluster, an M6+, or a raised volcano alert follows in the 0–48 hour or 2–7 day window after it — the same windows used in the 20-year test. These events are common, so some matches happen by pure chance. The tallies below show how many matches chance alone would give. Only a long record that beats chance by a wide margin would be worth a closer look, and even then it would not show cause.

Solar wind lulls

A lull is a sharp 24-hour drop in solar wind speed or dynamic pressure, or the first quiet (Kp ≤ 2) after a geomagnetic storm (Kp ≥ 5). The thresholds are the same ones used in the 20-year downtick test below, frozen in advance. A lull is flagged only after a window has fully elapsed, and only if a declustered M4.5+ quake cluster, an M6+, or a raised U.S. volcano alert falls in that window. Charge-then-release is a speed drop that followed three days of unusually fast wind.

Earthquakes × solar wind

Do solar wind spikes come before more earthquakes? Mainstream seismology has found no robust link between space weather and earthquakes. We test it anyway, in the open, with rules fixed before looking. Big quakes are followed by many aftershocks, so we count quakes two ways: raw (every M4.5+ quake) and declustered (aftershocks and foreshocks removed with the standard Gardner–Knopoff windows). The main test was chosen before running it: declustered M4.5+ quakes in the 48 hours after each solar wind spike.

20-year test: downticks and lulls (2006–2026)

Do drops, or the quiet after a storm, come before more earthquakes? The idea is a weather analogy: energy builds up, then a lull or release might trigger events. We tested four pre-set triggers (a 24-hour speed drop of at least 100 km/s, a sharp dynamic-pressure drop, the first Kp ≤ 2 after a Kp ≥ 5 storm, and a speed drop after three fast days) in two windows (0–48 hours and days 2–7). Eight tests, Bonferroni bar p < 0.00625. The main numbers are in the table; none passed the bar.
