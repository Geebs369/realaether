Randomness: cumulative deviation (GCP style)

Each pair of consecutive frames contributes a 10,000-bit trial of sensor-noise bits, debiased by comparing each pixel with itself one frame later (temporal von Neumann) and sampled across the whole sensor; frame z-scores are combined (Stouffer) into a minute Z. The line is the cumulative sum of (Z²−1), the Global Consciousness Project "netvar" statistic: chance wanders around zero like a random walk, so only a sustained trend (well beyond ±2√(2N)) is interesting. Gold vertical lines mark notable world events logged by the operator.

Randomness stats

"Raw" is the sensor-noise LSB before any processing; tiny biases there are expected from the ADC and are statistically huge with millions of bits. The debiased stream is what the GCP test uses. SHA-256 whitening hides everything, so it is only used for the random byte pool.

Pixelation test

Honest null hypothesis: the sensor is itself a square pixel grid behind a Bayer filter, so short tracks will cluster at 0°, 45°, 90° and 135°: that's an artifact of how we measure, not evidence of a pixelated universe. The interesting quantity is the 4-fold (grid) signal in long tracks, weighted by length, compared with what the short-track artifact predicts. A real "simulation grid" would have to show up in long tracks, stay put when the camera is rotated, and survive many weeks of data. Muons arrive mostly from overhead, so a 2-fold (single-direction) signal reflects how the camera is mounted, not new physics.
